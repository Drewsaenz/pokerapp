Positions

Your seat relative to the button decides how many players act after you. The more players behind you, the stronger your hand needs to be, because one of them will often have something better. Late position also means acting last after the flop, which is worth a lot.

Pre-flop order: UTG first, big blind last. After the flop: small blind first, button last. 6-handed: LJ, HJ, CO, BTN, SB, BB (the LJ is called UTG there).

Player types

Two questions sort almost every player. How many hands do they play? Tight players play few, loose players play many. Do they bet and raise, or check and call? Aggressive players drive the betting, passive players follow it. Answer both and you know how to play against them.

VPIP is how often a player voluntarily puts money in before the flop; PFR is how often they raise. Typical full-ring numbers; six-handed runs a bit higher. Your own VPIP is in the practice table's Session panel.

Rules of thumb

  • Play tighter up front, wider on the button. UTG opens about 15% of hands; the button opens about 45%.
  • Raise or fold, rarely limp. Raising first gives you two ways to win: everyone folds, or you have the best hand.
  • Facing a raise, re-raise or fold more than you call. Calling is the weakest option, especially with offsuit hands that are often outkicked (QJo, KTo, A9o).
  • Respect early-position raises. Their range is strong, so your continuing range has to be too.
  • The big blind defends widest. You already have a bet in and close the action, so the price is good.
  • Short stacks push or fold. Under about 15bb there's no room to raise and then fold. Shove the hands in the chart.
  • Tighten up near the money. On the bubble and at final tables, survival is worth more than chips, so call off your stack less often.
